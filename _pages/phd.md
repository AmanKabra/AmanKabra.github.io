A dissertation is the work of one person on paper, but never in practice. This is the acknowledgment section from my dissertation — because the people behind the work deserve to be on the website too.

---

To call this doctoral journey mine alone could not be further from the truth. Undergirding this journey is a constellation of people, and I want to take stock of my gratitude toward them here.

Professionally, I begin with Stephen Humphrey, my advisor. He is a remarkable scholar and an equally thoughtful mentor who gave me the patient latitude to find my research footing. He invested in me before there was anything to show for it. Margaret Luciano played a significant role — to say the least — in sculpting my research trajectory. I turned to her often, and she never turned me away. James LeBreton and Russell Barton provided a much-needed statistical foundation to my program of research through both coursework and coauthorships. Semin Park and Rui Zhong generously supported my dissertation and second-year-paper journeys, respectively. I also acknowledge Federico Aime and Maartje Schouten, whose theorizing I have learnt deeply from. I thank Madan Pillutla and Sarang Deo for steering me toward Penn State in the first place, and John Hollenbeck for building the multiteam systems lab infrastructure that gave me my first laboratory experience. Finally, I acknowledge the role of my seminar course instructors at Penn State who exposed me to the know-how of theorizing and the theories I now draw upon.

Personally, I thank my parents and my sister Aayushi — had they not given me the support to build an overseas research career in the United States, this journey would not have happened in the first place. A PhD is a bet with a long horizon and uncertain payoffs; they underwrote that bet without hesitation. And, Sakshi — the only person who closely traced (and lived) both the benefits and costs of this journey — for holding my hand through the ups, downs, and plateaus.
